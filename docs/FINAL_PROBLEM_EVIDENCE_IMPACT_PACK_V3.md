# TRUSTLIFY — FINAL PROBLEM, EVIDENCE & IMPACT PACK
## Regional-Round Evidence Baseline — V3

## 1. FINAL PROBLEM

> Pakistani students and ordinary internet users increasingly encounter opportunities and claims through websites, social media, messaging apps, screenshots, and third-party portals. Official Pakistani institutions and documented cases show that some of these claims can be impersonated, unauthorized, outdated, misleading, or designed to obtain money or personal data. Users therefore need a safer way to compare what they see online with authoritative, current, and public evidence before they click, apply, pay, or share information.

This is stronger than saying “students are constantly being scammed” because the research does not establish a national prevalence rate.

## 2. THE CORE PAIN

The user is trying to answer:

1. Is this source really who it claims to be?
2. Is the opportunity genuine?
3. Is the information still current?
4. Do credible sources agree?
5. Is the application/link safe?
6. Is it requesting unnecessary personal information or payment?
7. Does the opportunity actually fit me?
8. What should I do next?

Trustlify should therefore be positioned as an **evidence-driven decision assistant**, not a binary scam detector.

Why it matters:

- domain comparison
- look-alike detection

## 4. PROBLEM TYPES TRUSTLIFY SHOULD HANDLE

1. **IMPERSONATED** — real organization, fake account/domain/application
2. **OUTDATED** — genuine information, no longer current
3. **CONFLICTING** — credible sources disagree
4. **UNVERIFIED** — insufficient evidence
5. **HIGH RISK** — strong evidence-backed warning signals
6. **UNSUITABLE** — genuine but poor personal fit

Do not reduce all of these to REAL/FAKE.

## 5. IMPORTANT NEW FEATURE: ACTION PLAN

Trustlify must answer:

> What should I do next?

Examples:

- Unverified scholarship → do not submit sensitive data; check official portal/contact
- Look-alike domain → close it; navigate manually to official domain
- Password entered → change password and enable 2FA
- OTP shared → treat as urgent and secure the account
- Payment made → preserve transaction evidence and contact the relevant payment provider/authority
- Fake social account → save evidence and report through the platform
- Genuine but expired → do not apply; find the latest official announcement

This turns Trustlify from a classifier into a decision assistant.

## 6. PRIVACY GUARD

Trustlify itself can receive:
- CNIC/passport
- bank information
- OTP/passwords
- phone/email
- student cards
- application forms

MVP direction:
- detect obvious sensitive patterns
- show a redaction warning
- minimize storage
- never encourage users to paste passwords/OTPs

Long-term:
temporary processing + explicit saving controls.

## 7. PERSONAL FIT

Separate:
- Authenticity
- Currentness
- Eligibility
- Relevance
- Financial suitability
- Safety

Do not collapse them into one unexplained score.

Possible user-facing match:
- Education: Match
- Location: Match
- Skills: Strong
- Experience: Unknown
- Fees: Needs review
- Safety: No obvious red flags found

## 8. OPPORTUNITY FAIRNESS / COST

Check:
- upfront fees
- training fees
- refundable deposits
- paid certificates
- unpaid trial work
- recruitment fees
- mandatory purchases
- recruit-other-people requirements
- excessive data collection

Potential result:
> Authentic organization, but financially questionable opportunity.

## 9. IDENTITY RELATIONSHIP GRAPH

Eventually connect:

Organization
→ official website
→ official social account
→ email domain
→ application domain
→ recruiter identity
→ payment recipient

This is stronger than a simple domain check.

## 10. CURRENTNESS / RECYCLED OPPORTUNITIES

Compare:
- original publication date
- update date
- deadline
- current application status
- inactive/archive state
- newer announcement
- changed requirements

Strong result:
> Genuine source, but no longer current.

## 11. EVIDENCE PACK / REPORTING

Long-term:
- original URL
- screenshot
- organization
- dates
- evidence
- official warnings
- timeline
- classification
- recommended reporting route

For regional round, a simple “Save Evidence” capability is enough.

## 12. “I ALREADY ACTED” MODE

Future/finale feature:
- clicked link
- submitted email/phone
- uploaded CNIC/passport
- entered password
- shared OTP
- paid money
- downloaded a file

Then provide urgency-based next steps.

Do not build this before the core investigation is reliable.

## 13. SCREENSHOT PROVENANCE

Do not tell users:
> “This screenshot looks fake.”

Instead:
1. extract text
2. identify organization
3. search exact wording
4. compare official pages
5. compare dates/deadlines
6. check URLs/domains
7. identify mismatches
8. report what was and was not verified

Strong wording:
> “The screenshot resembles the organization’s branding, but we found no matching official announcement. Treat it as unverified.”

## 14. PUBLIC HUMAN EVIDENCE

Use:
- public reports
- public comments where accessible
- forums
- fact-checks
- news reports
- user-submitted evidence

Classify as:
- LEAD
- CORROBORATED
- CONFLICTING
- UNCONFIRMED

Do not count comments as a truth score.

## 15. “SEARCH IS NOT VERIFICATION”

Research in the report shows that online searching does not automatically guarantee correct verification.

Trustlify should show:
- source authority
- direct support
- currentness
- conflicts
- missing evidence
- why the final state was assigned

That is a central reason Trustlify is more than a search box.

## 16. IMPACT / GRAPH STRATEGY

Do NOT use an unsupported statement such as:
“Students spend 70% of their time scrolling.”

The research found no reliable source directly measuring:
- Pakistani students
- opportunity verification time
- number of sources checked
- eligibility-checking effort

Instead use three panels:

### Panel 1 — Pakistan digital context
Use sourced context such as:
- 116M internet users, Jan 2025
- 66.9M social-media user identities, Jan 2025
- 200M telecom subscribers, June 2025
- 150M broadband subscribers, June 2025

Caption:
> Pakistan's information environment is large and highly connected.

These figures do not prove student behavior.

### Panel 2 — Manual verification journey
Post → Search → Official website → Another source → Deadline → Eligibility → Reviews → Decision

Caption:
> Trust often requires stitching evidence together across multiple places.

### Panel 3 — Trustlify pilot
Once tested, measure:
- time to responsible decision
- steps
- authoritative sources surfaced
- conflicts identified
- evidence coverage
- user confidence before/after

Label:
> Trustlify pilot findings — not national prevalence data.

## 18. CLAIMS WE CAN SAFELY MAKE

Strong:
- Pakistani institutions have warned about fake websites, impersonation, unauthorized links, fraudulent recruitment, scholarship-related fraud, and manipulated social-media content.
- Students/youth can encounter opportunity-related fraud through multiple channels.
- Authenticity is only one part of the decision; currentness, source identity, eligibility, relevance, cost, and safety also matter.
- Pakistan has a large digital information environment.

Not yet proven nationally:
- Most students encounter scams.
- Students spend X% of time verifying opportunities.
- Instagram is the main discovery source.
- Students frequently lose money.
- Trustlify detects every scam.
- Trustlify is 100% accurate.

## 19. FINAL WEBSITE MESSAGE

### Information is everywhere. Confidence isn't.

Trustlify investigates online claims against authoritative and public evidence before you click, apply, pay, or share personal data.

Student layer:
> Not only “Is it real?” — also “Does it fit me, is it current, and what should I do next?”

## 20. FINAL PRODUCT POSITIONING

> **Trustlify is an evidence-driven decision assistant that helps people investigate online opportunities before they click, apply, pay, or share personal information.**

Its strongest value:
> It checks whether the source is authorized, whether the information is current, whether the user appears eligible, whether the terms are safe, and what the user should do next.

## 21. CORE LIMITATION

Trustlify cannot guarantee truth.

Its quality depends on:
- available sources
- source accessibility
- source quality
- currentness
- model interpretation
- user-provided data

Design principle:
> Accuracy > confidence.
> Evidence > assumptions.
> Unverified > invented certainty.
